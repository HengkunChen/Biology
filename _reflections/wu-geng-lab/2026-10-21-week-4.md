## One idea I understand better now

### Catalytic antibodies (abzymes)

1. Synthesize a stable transition-state analog (TSA) as a hapten.
2. Conjugate it to a carrier protein (KLH or BSA) and inject with an adjuvant. The carrier gives T-cell help, which drives affinity maturation.
3. Make monoclonal antibodies by hybridoma technology.
4. Screen clones for TSA binding, then for actual catalytic activity. Many binders are inactive, so screening is the bottleneck.

**Why they are useful**

- Catalyze reactions with no natural enzyme (e.g., Diels-Alder).
- Can be highly selective, including for stereoisomers.
- Therapeutic uses: prodrug activation at tumors, degrading cocaine, cleaving harmful targets.
- Antibodies are stable, engineerable scaffolds with long half-lives.
- They tested transition-state theory.

**Limitations**

- Rate accelerations are typically 10³ to 10⁶, far below natural enzymes (up to 10¹⁷), because antibodies are selected for binding rather than turnover and lack precisely placed catalytic groups and dynamics.
- Product inhibition can occur.
- Improvements include bait-and-switch haptens, reactive immunization and directed evolution.
- Directed evolution and computational enzyme design are now often more practical for novel catalysts.
