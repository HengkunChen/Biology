## One idea I understand better now

### How gene therapy works

- **Addition vs. editing:** gene addition delivers a spare working copy and cuts nothing. Gene editing (CRISPR) changes the existing DNA.
- **Delivery:** viral vectors (adenovirus, AAV, lentivirus) or non-viral methods (lipid nanoparticles, electroporation).
- **Finding the right spot:** a ~20-letter guide RNA matches only the target sequence, and Cas9 cuts there. The cell then repairs the cut either by NHEJ, which disables a gene, or by HDR, which uses a supplied template to replace the sequence.
- **Targeting the right cells:**
  - local injection
  - viral tropism (which cell types a virus prefers)
  - tissue-specific promoters
  - ex vivo editing (take cells out, edit them, return them), which is how CAR-T and Casgevy work
  - guide RNA specificity
