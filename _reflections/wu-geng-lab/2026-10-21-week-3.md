## One idea I understand better now

### Conjugation (coupling) summary

Chemically joining two molecules.

In ADCs, a linker-payload (toxic drug) is attached to an antibody, which acts as the homing device that binds a cancer cell’s surface protein.

**The coupling methods, from least to most precise**

- **Lysine (amine) coupling:** targets about 80+ surface lysines. The result is a random mixture with an average DAR around 3.5. Example: Kadcyla.
- **Native cysteine coupling:** interchain disulfides are reduced to free thiols, then a maleimide linker attaches. The result is a mixture of 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 drugs. Examples: Adcetris, Enhertu.
- **THIOMAB (reactive cysteine):** cysteines are engineered in at chosen positions. The result is uniform, typically 2 drugs per antibody. Developed at Genentech.
- **Unnatural amino acid:** an engineered codon plus a unique reactive group, joined by click-type chemistry. Very precise, but complex to make.
- **Enzyme-catalyzed:** enzymes such as transglutaminase, sortase and formylglycine-generating enzyme recognize specific sites. Site-specific and done under mild conditions.

**Why precision matters**

- **DAR trade-off:** too few drugs means weak killing, while too many makes the ADC clump and clear from the blood faster.
- **Stability:** maleimide bonds can slowly release the payload early, harming healthy tissue.
- **Uniformity:** site-specific methods give every molecule the same drug count and position, which improves predictability and the safety margin.
- **Linker type:** cleavable linkers are snipped inside the cell, while non-cleavable ones (Kadcyla) release the drug only after the antibody is degraded in the lysosome.
